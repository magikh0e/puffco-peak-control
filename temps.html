Dab temperature guide

Temperature changes both flavor and effect. Lower temperatures keep more of the terpene flavor with a gentler hit; higher temperatures hit harder but burn the flavor off. Below are the boiling points of common terpenes and cannabinoids, and the usual heat ranges for each dab style — the numbers you dial in on your Peak Pro — or load as ready-made profiles from the app’s Library tab.

Dab styles

Low-temp

400–500°F

Coolest and most flavorful. Thin, tasty vapor that keeps the terpenes intact, with a lighter, cleaner high. Leaves more oil behind in the dish.

Mid-range

500–545°F

The balance point — solid flavor and a fuller effect, with thicker vapor. A good default if you’re not sure where to start.

Standard

545–570°F

Bigger clouds and a stronger hit, with the flavor starting to drop off. Vaporizes the oil fast and clears the dish.

Hot

570–600°F

Harshest and most intense. Maximum vapor and effect, almost no flavor, and hot enough to scorch the oil. Hard on the throat.

Boiling points

  • Caryophyllene266°F
  • Pinene311°F
  • THC315°F
  • Myrcene334°F
  • Ocimene347°F
  • Limonene349°F
  • CBD356°F
  • Terpinolene365°F
  • Humulene388°F
  • Linalool388°F

Boiling points vary a few degrees between sources — treat these as starting points and trust your own taste. Terpenes boil off first, which is why the flavorful dabs sit at the cool end. For what each of the big five terpenes does, see Tropical Roots Maui's terpene guide.
